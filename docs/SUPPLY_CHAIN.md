# The Pakistani bedsheet supply chain: a working guide

*For the family business in Ichra, Lahore. Figures are indicative ranges as of 2025–26. Before relying on any number, check it against current market rates from your suppliers.*

---

## 1. The big picture

Pakistan is one of the few countries that has **every stage of the cotton textile chain inside its borders**. Textiles make up about 55–60% of national exports, and home textiles (bedsheets, towels, linen) are one of the largest categories. Faisalabad alone is often called the "Manchester of Pakistan".

```
 Cotton farm ─► Ginning ─► Spinning ─► Weaving ─► Processing ─► CUT & STITCH ─► Packing ─► Distribution
 (Punjab/Sindh)  (bales)    (yarn)     (grey cloth) (dye/print)  (Ichra units)              (wholesale/retail)
                                                                 ▲ we are here
```

A typical Ichra manufacturer works at **stages 6–8**. They buy finished (processed) fabric, or buy grey cloth and pay a mill to process it on job-work. Then they cut, stitch, pack and sell.

---

## 2. Stage by stage

| # | Stage | Where | What happens | Who you deal with | Lead time |
|---|-------|-------|--------------|-------------------|-----------|
| 1 | **Cotton farming** | South Punjab (Multan, Bahawalpur, Rahim Yar Khan, Vehari), Sindh (Sanghar, Hyderabad, Mirpurkhas) | Sown Apr–May, picked Aug–Dec | Not directly | Seasonal |
| 2 | **Ginning** | Near the fields | Fibre separated from seed and pressed into bales of about 170 kg (375 lb) | Not directly | — |
| 3 | **Spinning** | Faisalabad, Multan, Karachi, Lahore | Bales become yarn. Counts: 20s/30s for everyday sheets, 40s+ for fine fabric. PSF (polyester staple fibre) is blended in for poly-cotton | Yarn brokers (if you buy grey cloth yourself) | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | **Weaving** | **Faisalabad** (the main hub), also Lahore and Kasur | Wide-width looms (90"–120") weave grey/kora cloth. Constructions: plain/percale (e.g. 144–200 TC) and sateen (250–400 TC) | Faisalabad cloth dealers and weaving units | 2–4 weeks |
| 5 | **Processing** | Faisalabad, Lahore (Sundar Industrial Estate, Kot Lakhpat) | Desizing, scouring, bleaching, mercerising, dyeing (reactive dyes for cotton), rotary/digital printing, softening, sanforizing (pre-shrink) | Processing mills on **job-work** (you supply the cloth and pay per metre) | 1–3 weeks |
| 6 | **Cut & stitch** | **Ichra**, and also Shah Alam / Azam markets, Faisalabad | Laying, cutting, hemming, pillow covers, fitted-sheet elastics | Your own karigars (tailors) or contract stitching on a per-piece rate | Days |
| 7 | **QC & packing** | Same unit | Checking, folding, PVC bag or box, label, carton | Packaging suppliers (Lahore) | Days |
| 8 | **Distribution** | All of Pakistan | Wholesale markets, retailers, hotels, hospitals, online sellers (Daraz, Instagram), export | Goods transport (Daewoo cargo, local "adda" trucks), couriers (TCS, Leopards, M&P) | 1–5 days |

## 3. Where the money goes (King bedsheet set, illustration)

These are rough proportions for a 180 TC printed cotton King set (sheet 108×108" plus 2 pillow covers):

| Cost component | Share of ex-factory cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Processed fabric (~6.5–7 m at 108"+ width) | **60–70%** | The biggest driver by far. Follows cotton, yarn, gas and electricity prices and the PKR/USD rate |
| Stitching labour | 6–10% | Per-piece karigar rate |
| Thread, elastic, labels | 2–4% | |
| Packaging (bag/box, insert, carton) | 4–8% | Boxes cost much more than PVC bags |
| Wastage (cutting, rejects) | 3–5% | Good marker planning reduces it |
| Overheads (rent, power, transport in) | 5–10% | |

**Typical markups (indicative):** manufacturer → wholesaler 10–20%; wholesaler → retailer 15–25%; retailer → customer 30–60%. **Selling direct** (your website, WhatsApp, Instagram) lets you keep part of those margins, and that is the main commercial reason this website exists.

## 4. Key risks and how to manage them

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Cotton/yarn price swings** | Fabric is about two-thirds of cost. A bad crop or import changes can move prices 10–30% in a season | Buy fabric ahead in low season (Dec–Feb, after the harvest). Keep price lists dated. Quote validity of 7–15 days |
| **Energy costs** (gas/electricity at mills) | Push up processing rates | Keep 2–3 processing mills on your list and compare job-work rates |
| **PKR depreciation** | Raises the cost of imported dyes, chemicals and polyester | Pass through quickly with dated price lists |
| **Quality variation** (GSM, shrinkage, colour fastness) | Returns and lost dealers | Check GSM and thread count on every lot and do a wash test. Insist on sanforized fabric |
| **Credit / udhaar** | Wholesale customers often pay late | Set credit limits, ask for advance on new accounts, and track it in the admin panel notes |
| **Copying of designs** | Prints get copied in the market within weeks | Launch prints in small batches and keep refreshing |

## 5. Seasonality (demand)

- **Wedding season** (Oct–Feb, plus after Eid): strong demand for comforter sets, velvet and dowry (jahez) packages.
- **Summer** (Apr–Aug): light cotton sheets and single sheets.
- **Ramadan / Eid**: retail spike, so stock up 4–6 weeks early.
- **Winter** (Nov–Jan): razai covers, flannel and heavier sets.
- **Hotels and hospitals**: steady all year, often through yearly contracts or tenders.

---

## 6. Growth opportunities

1. **Direct-to-consumer online**: website, WhatsApp catalogue, Instagram/TikTok and Daraz. The margin captured is the retailer's 30–60%.
2. **Institutional contracts**: hotels, guest houses, hospitals and hostels. Steady volume. Needs percale/poly-cotton and reliable delivery.
3. **Private label**: stitching for new online bedding brands (many startups need a manufacturer).
4. **Export (later)**: Middle East and UK diaspora markets, through B2B platforms. This needs certifications (OEKO-TEX, BSCI) and consistent quality.
5. **Backward integration**: buying grey cloth from Faisalabad and processing on job-work instead of buying finished fabric. This can save 8–15% on fabric, but needs working capital and know-how.

---

## 7. Supplier checklist

When qualifying a fabric or processing supplier, ask for or check:

- [ ] Construction (e.g. 20×20 / 60×60 or 30×30 / 76×68) and **actual thread count**
- [ ] **GSM** (weight per m²) and width after finishing
- [ ] Shrinkage after wash (target < 3%) and sanforizing
- [ ] Colour fastness to washing and rubbing (target grade 4 or better)
- [ ] Minimum order, lead time, and price validity
- [ ] Payment terms (advance, or credit days)
- [ ] Sample yardage before a bulk order

## 8. Glossary (English ↔ market terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Grey / kora cloth | Unprocessed woven fabric straight from the loom |
| Job-work | Paying a mill to process *your* cloth (per-metre rate) |
| TC (thread count) | Warp + weft threads per square inch |
| Percale | Plain weave, crisp and matte (hotel sheets) |
| Sateen | 4-over-1 weave, smooth with a sheen (premium sheets) |
| Count (20s, 30s, 40s) | Yarn fineness: higher means finer |
| Sanforized | Mechanically pre-shrunk |
| Reactive dyes | Dyes that bond chemically with cotton, giving good wash fastness |
| Karigar | Skilled stitching worker |
| MOQ | Minimum order quantity |
